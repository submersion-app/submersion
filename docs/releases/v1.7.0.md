# 🎉 Submersion v1.7.0 Release Notes

v1.7.0 is the largest release yet, and it reaches in every direction at once. Big logbooks that had become painfully slow are now fast. Your photos and videos can live in cloud storage you own and appear on every one of your devices. There is a new post-dive safety suite, a review inbox that finds suspicious imported data, 3D views of your dives, a weight planner that learns from your own history, optional password protection for both sync and backups, and a planner that finally uses the whole window. Around those headliners are deeper equipment and certification tracking, a new import path for Aqualung DiverLog users, Shearwater Perdix 3 downloads, and a long list of fixes. Everything upgrades automatically the first time you open the app.

---

## ✨ New and improved

### Large logbooks are dramatically faster

If you dive a lot, Submersion had grown sluggish — searches that dragged on for twenty seconds, charts that took minutes to redraw. That is fixed. Searches that used to crawl now return almost instantly, opening a dive is quick, and toggling chart overlays is snappy again. There is nothing for you to do: every existing logbook speeds itself up the first time you open this version, and stays responsive as it keeps growing.

### Your photos and videos, on every device

Submersion can now keep your actual photos and videos in cloud storage that you own, so a photo you attach on your phone shows up on your Mac and your tablet — and survives losing the phone. This replaces the old, unreliable approach of trying to re-match against each device's photo library.

- **A photo store you control.** Point Submersion at your own cloud storage under Settings → Media Storage (it can reuse your existing sync settings). Photos upload on their own when you attach them and download when you need them, and the same photo is never uploaded twice.
- **Connected Accounts.** A new Photos & Media hub keeps your photo sources, your storage, and your accounts in one place, with a guided checklist and a "finish setting up this device" card that walks a new phone or computer through reconnecting. Your existing setup carries over with no need to sign in again.
- **Choose how much to upload.** Each device picks an upload quality for photos and for videos — Original, High, Balanced, or Small — so you can trade a little quality for a lot less cloud storage and data usage. Your original files on the device are never altered, and a photo that is already small enough uploads as-is.
- **Adobe Lightroom auto-linking.** Connect a Lightroom cloud catalog and photos and videos taken during a dive attach themselves, with clear matches linked automatically and close calls offered as one-tap suggestions. (The Lightroom options are hidden by default in this release while we complete Adobe's review.)
- **Real video thumbnails on desktop.** Videos added from your files used to show a plain movie icon on Mac, Windows, and Linux; they now show a real preview frame.
- **A "not backed up" marker.** Photo tiles show a small cloud icon when an item hasn't been uploaded yet, so a local-only photo is never silently left behind.

### A post-dive safety suite

A new, deliberately non-judgmental set of safety features.

- **Post-dive safety review.** After a dive with a recorded profile, Submersion quietly looks it over and notes things worth a glance in plain, neutral language — a rapid ascent ("ascent exceeded 12 m/min for 40 s"), a missed or shortened deco stop, a skipped safety stop, a sawtooth profile, or a high surfacing gradient factor. Findings show as a calm section on the dive page (no alarm-red) and a small dot on the dive list, and you can dismiss any of them. You control it all — a master switch, per-check toggles, and an "Analyze all dives" button — under Settings → Decompression → Safety review.
- **No-fly countdown and safety hub.** A running "time to fly" countdown after your last dive, gathered with the other safety tools in one place.
- **Offline emergency card.** A card you can fill in and reach even with no signal.
- **Near-miss log.** Aviation-style, non-punitive incident logging — pick a category and severity, and jot down what happened, what contributed, and what would help next time. Near-miss reports stay between your own devices and your backups; they are never written into any export or shared file. You can log one straight from a dive, and it stays even if that dive is later deleted.
- **Pre-dive checklists.** Reusable checklists to run before you get in.

### The Data Quality Assistant

A review inbox that flags suspicious imported dive data and offers guided, undoable fixes. It watches for clock and timezone slips, gaps in the profile, impossible depth spikes and ascent rates, odd temperatures and pressures, gas and MOD mismatches, pressure recorded against the wrong cylinder, conflicting computer sources, likely duplicates, and dives that were probably split by accident. It checks both new imports and your entire existing logbook on demand, so problems buried in years of old dives get caught too. Each finding offers the right fix — fold a duplicate, merge a split, shift an import's clock, reassign a cylinder's pressure, or clean up a noisy profile — and everything you do can be undone.

### See your dives in 3D

A new set of 3D views you can spin, zoom, and scrub through:

- **Single dive** — your depth-and-time profile as a ribbon you can color by any metric, with temperature bands, a deco ceiling, and markers for events, photos, and gas switches.
- **Tissue saturation** — a moving landscape of your sixteen tissue compartments across a day's repetitive dives, with a helium view for trimix.
- **Career terrain** — many dives laid side by side, reached from a dive site's page.
- **Spatial seascape** — a Suunto-style swim path traced from your compass headings between your entry and exit GPS points, over a suggested seafloor. It is honest about itself: "estimated path" and "synthesized seafloor" labels are always shown.

### A weight planner that learns from your dives

The old rule-of-thumb weight calculator is replaced by a personalized **Weight Planner**. Set up the rig you plan to dive — gear or a saved equipment set, cylinders, water type — and get a lead recommendation, both a total and where to place it, based on your own logged dives. It tells you how confident it is (and how many dives that's from), and updates live as you swap gear. What teaches it is a simple "How was your weighting?" question on the dive form — felt right, overweighted, or underweighted. With no history yet, it falls back to sensible physics and whatever buoyancy figures you've entered for your gear.

Building on the same idea, the **Buoyancy Digital Twin** models your buoyancy through a whole dive, so you can see why a given setup was hard to hold at the last stop. It accounts for your cylinders getting lighter as you breathe them down, your wetsuit compressing with depth, and your wing or drysuit — then compares the ideal amount of lead against what you actually carried, with a plain-language read-out on the dive page and a what-if panel to try changes.

### Optional password protection

Two independent, opt-in ways to lock things down, each with its own password and a printable recovery code:

- **Encrypted cloud sync.** Turn it on and everything Submersion stores in your cloud provider becomes unreadable to anyone without your password. Your other devices ask for the password once, then sync normally.
- **Encrypted backups.** Set a password and every backup the app makes — the automatic local ones, manual Save-to-File and Share exports, and cloud uploads — is protected. This is separate from sync encryption, so you can use either or both, and a protected cloud backup can be restored on a new device with just the password.

### The planner gets the whole window

The dive planner's core — the live profile canvas, real closed-circuit planning, contingencies, and the printable slate — arrived in v1.6.0; this release rebuilds how it looks. A cleaner, sharper profile chart shows deco stops, the no-go ceiling, gas-switch flags, and your mean depth clearly, even on a long trimix ascent. The planner now spreads across the whole window: editor, chart, and results side by side on a wide screen, and a chart with a tidy tab deck on a phone. The results start with the numbers you reach for first — runtime, TTS or NDL, CNS, and warnings.

Also new here: a **stepped deco-stop band on the dive profile chart** for logged dives, switchable between the app's own numbers and your computer's; and the ability to **delete a plan right from the canvas** with an undo, plus a trash button on saved plans and a fix so a saved plan closes with a single back press.

### Correct decompression for logged closed-circuit dives

Logged CCR dives used to have their decompression figured as if you were breathing your first cylinder open-circuit, which understated the obligation badly — one 44 m dive showed 17 minutes to surface where it should have shown 24. Submersion now models the rebreather loop at its setpoints, so the deco and time-to-surface on the dive page match what a proper CCR plan would give.

### A proper Gauge dive mode

Bottom-timer dives that record only depth and time now have a first-class **Gauge** mode. A gauge dive no longer shows decompression, ppO₂, CNS, MOD, or gas figures it can't really know, and it no longer counts as an "Air" dive in your gas statistics. A dive downloaded from a computer set to gauge mode is recognized automatically. As part of this, dives downloaded from a computer in CCR or SCR mode now keep that mode instead of arriving as open-circuit and needing to be set by hand.

### Configurable CNS calculation

You can now choose how CNS oxygen toxicity is calculated: the classic NOAA stepped table, a Shearwater-style method, or a Subsurface-style one, with a short explanation of each in Settings → Decompression. **The new default is the Shearwater-style method**, which gives slightly lower CNS values than before; if you'd rather see your previous numbers, choose "classic."

### Equipment tracking goes deeper

- **Details that fit each item.** Instead of one flat set of fields, each type of gear now offers the details that actually matter for it — a wetsuit gets size, thickness, and style; a cylinder gets volume, working pressure, material, valve, and visual/hydro inspection dates; a regulator gets DIN-or-yoke and a cold-water rating — plus your own custom fields on anything. There's a new "Dives by Suit Thickness" chart, and you can filter dives by suit thickness. Your existing gear details carry over untouched.
- **A gear service ledger.** One service reminder per item becomes as many as an item needs — separate hydro, visual, and O2-clean schedules on a cylinder, "annual or 100 dives, whichever comes first" on a regulator, battery reminders on computers and transmitters. Best of all, it warns you *before a trip* rather than at the fill station: a banner flags any gear that will come due before the trip ends.
- **Default and geofenced equipment sets.** Mark one set as your default, and attach locations to any set so the right kit is suggested based on where a dive happened. It only ever suggests — it never overwrites gear you've already chosen.

### Certifications, buddies, and roles

- **Certification levels that match the agency.** The level list now depends on the agency you pick — the proper CMAS star grades, BSAC, GUE, and the technical agencies — instead of one generic list for everyone. Both forms now ask for the agency first.
- **Multiple certifications per buddy.** A buddy can hold a full list of certs — a main level plus specialties — just like your own profile.
- **A course requirement tracker.** Training courses can track countable requirements — Advanced Open Water's five adventure dives, Rescue prerequisites, and the like — against your logbook, with starter templates and one-tap dive crediting.
- **Dive roles.** Roles are now yours to extend: nine built-in ones (including Rear Guard, Support Diver, and Safety Diver) plus any custom roles you add. You can also record your *own* role on a dive.
- **Buddy and Dive Master columns** in the dives table now show the people you recorded (they had been coming up blank).

### New ways to get dives in, and new computers to get them from

- **Aqualung DiverLog import.** Import DiverLog+ data (`.zxu`/`.zxl` files), including the rich Aqualung details other tools skip — site names and GPS, cylinder pressures and gas mixes, ratings, and the computer's identity. A DiveCloud export ZIP imports in one step and attaches its photos to the right dives automatically.
- **Shearwater Perdix 3 downloads.** The Perdix 3 needed new download support to actually pull dives; it now works, and keeps the oldest-first Shearwater behavior from earlier releases.
- **Compass headings** are now captured from computers that record them, feeding the new 3D swim-path view.

### Dive detail and navigation

- **Previous / Next dive.** Step through your dives right from the dive page — buttons in the header, or the arrow keys on desktop — in exactly the order your list is sorted and filtered. Paired with **scroll-position memory**, so scrolling to a section and stepping to the next dive keeps you on the same section for easy comparison.
- **Side-by-side cards on wide screens.** The dive page now pairs Details with Conditions, and Buddies with Signatures, instead of one long column.
- **Jump from a dive to its site and back** on desktop without losing your place in the dive list.
- **A dive-computer overlay on photos and videos.** In the photo viewer you can bring up a draggable, translucent Perdix-style computer face that replays your readings in time with a video, or shows the reading at the moment a photo was taken — depth, NDL (switching to a stop under deco), time, temperature, gas, tank pressure, CNS, and ppO₂, all in your units.
- **The interactive trip story.** A trip's Overview is now a day-by-day story with a map that follows you along as you scroll — and it covers every kind of trip, whether planned, past, or in progress.

### A refreshed edit form and setup wizard

The dive and site edit forms finish their redesign onto one clean, consistent look, with cylinders shown as tidy rows that open an editor when you tap them. The first-run setup wizard reads as more general-purpose and now floats as a translucent card over the animated ocean background.

### Smaller touches

- **Water type flows from a site to the dive.** Set salt, fresh, or brackish once on a dive site, and every dive you assign to it picks it up — no more re-entering it each time.
- **A reset-to-north button** appears on any map once you've rotated it.
- **The Settings "Cloud Sync" entry is now "Database Cloud Sync"** to tell it apart from the new photo storage.

## 🐞 Bug fixes

### Restored or reset data no longer reappears or vanishes

Restoring your library through the setup wizard could wrongly say "no library found" even though your dives had actually come down — that's fixed. And "Reset Database" used to quietly undo itself when sync re-downloaded everything on the next launch; a reset now sticks, while leaving your cloud copy intact for when you reconnect.

### Restoring a large backup no longer shows errors on every page

Restoring a large backup could briefly leave every page showing a red error even though the restore itself was fine. Pages now stay working while the restore finishes.

### Encrypted sync fixes on a second device

A second device joining a protected library would accept the password but then keep asking for it on every launch; it now stays unlocked. Two crashes when restoring a protected backup — one from backup settings, one from the setup wizard — are also fixed.

### Photos and videos attach correctly

- Depth and time for a linked photo now travel with it across your devices, instead of showing up blank on a second device.
- On Mac, Windows, and Linux, photos and videos added from your files now match to the right dive by when they were taken (including iPhone HEIC photos), rather than not matching at all.
- A timezone slip that pinned some file-imported photos to the very start of a dive is fixed.
- Photos that got stuck showing "Waiting," and transfers stuck showing "Uploading" forever, now clear correctly.

### Dive profile chart fixes

- The shaded fill under the ceiling line, and the new deco-stop band, now actually appear.
- Every line on the chart now reaches the left edge instead of starting a moment late.
- A stray sliver of the wrong gas at the very start of the gas strip is gone.

### Other fixes

- **Bottom time no longer imports equal to your total dive time.** Subsurface, MacDive, and CSV imports could set the two equal; bottom time is now worked out properly from the profile.
- **3D views no longer spin forever** on some older dives.
- **Safety-review findings show up right away** after a sync or an "Analyze all dives" run, instead of only after a restart.
- **GPS coordinates carry across** when you fold one dive onto another.
- **Android Shearwater downloads that failed while asking to pair** now recover on their own and download normally.
- **Wording fixes:** built-in dive type names are now translated, the German app uses "AMV" rather than "SAC," and the weather section is fully translated.

## 🔧 Under the hood

Everything upgrades automatically the first time you open this version, and the app now double-checks your data is in good shape every time it launches, so a library that came from a backup or a sync always lands in the right state.

Cloud sync is more robust: deleting a dive now reliably stays deleted across all your devices, and a device that's been offline for a long time can rejoin without resurrecting old data or losing the dives it logged while away. Your photo storage also tidies up after itself, so deleting a dive removes its photos from the cloud and you don't pay for storage you can't see. And, as always, every new bit of text is translated into all ten supported languages.
